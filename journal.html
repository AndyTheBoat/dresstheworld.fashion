Collection 001 / China / A personal diary

The Shanghai
Diary.

I went looking for a silhouette. A skyscraper gave me one.

I'm 23, Swiss-Chinese, a model and fashion designer. Happily single, currently very involved with silver fabric. Here is my collection, from the first skyline reference to its imagined evenings out.

Chapter 01 / 1. Shanghai Tower

A skyline worth looking up for.

I began with the tower's twist, its blue glass, and the way the same shape changes after dark. These references and mood-board variations are where my Shanghai collection starts.

Mood-board variations · 4 images

Chapter 02 / 2. Color Palette

My kind of blue hour.

I wanted silver that feels cool, blue that feels deep, and just enough warmth to keep everything human. This is my little colour agreement before the fabric negotiations begin.

Chapter 03 / 3. Materials and Textures

Can a skyline feel soft?

I brought reflective surfaces, sheer layers, beads, and satin onto the table. I want the feeling of glass with something gentler underneath.

Chapter 04 / 4. Dress concept

Several dresses. One obsession.

I tried sheaths, pleats, drapes, and a peplum before choosing the window spiral. Paper is a wonderful place to change my mind.

Chapter 05 / 5. Glass Facade Pattern

Window shopping, literally.

I followed the facade's grids and curves into sample studies, then into the bodice. The aim is a connection you can feel without needing an architectural tour.

Chapter 06a / 6a. Initial Design Without Diagonal Rib

Before the silver line.

I wanted the draping to work before asking one dramatic detail to carry the look. This quieter stage lets me see the shape clearly.

Chapter 06b / 6b. Design With Diagonal Silver Rib

The detail that changed the mood.

I added the silver rib across the drape and suddenly the tower's movement felt clearer. Sometimes the answer is one well-placed line.

Chapter 07 / 7. Hat Design

A little height of my own.

I wanted a folded headpiece that echoes the spiral without competing with the dress. Consider it the collection's smallest skyline.

Chapter 08 / 8. Fingerless Gloves Design

Geometry, with fingers free.

I carried the winding lines onto sheer gloves. I like accessories that join the story and still leave room to hold a pencil.

Chapter 09 / 9. Scarf

A softer plot twist.

I let the scarf bring a little movement to all that precision. The window grid stays, but this time it gets to float.

Chapter 10 / 10. Nail Art

The skyline at my fingertips.

I took the palette and window rhythm all the way down to the nails. It is a tiny canvas with an unexpectedly strong opinion.

Chapter 11 / 11. Lace-Up Shoes

A grounded kind of drama.

I used pointed toes, translucent-looking panels, and winding laces to carry the idea to the floor. The last detail should feel as considered as the first.

Chapter 12 / 12. Complete Look

Everyone finally gets dressed.

I put the whole ensemble on the mannequin to check the conversation between the dress and accessories. It is a useful pause before adding a person.

Chapter 14 / 14. Model Casting and Final Look

Finding the collection’s presence.

I compared ten casting visuals in the same black dress and atelier setting. Expressions and posture change how I read a silhouette; the board helps me see those differences together.

Chapter 15 / 15. Model Makeup

A little glass, around the eyes.

I translated the cool palette into a face chart, application study, and finished look. I wanted the eyes to echo the collection while the rest of the face stayed soft.

Chapter 16 / 16. Final Look in the Atelier

The full picture, at last.

I brought the dress, makeup, and accessories into one atelier image. This is the moment when all my separate pages begin to read like one collection.

Chapter 17 / 17. Final Look - Atelier Day and Night

The dress has evening plans.

I imagined the collection beyond the worktable, in lounges, a cocktail bar, and beside a rooftop piano. With or without the accessories, I wanted the dress to feel part of a conversation.
